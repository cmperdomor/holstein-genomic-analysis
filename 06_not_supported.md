# Conclusions this analysis does NOT support

- That variability *changed* for any single subindex. No Brown-Forsythe test survives FDR
  (all FDR > 0.21).
- That the reproduction lower tail is *robustly* declining. The point slope is negative but
  the sire-clustered bootstrap CI includes zero ([-12.2, +6.5]).
- That the most variable subindex (EI) is the "best selection opportunity." Its width is
  driven by a weak lower tail, not by strong candidates.
- That rising cross-subindex dispersion is *bad*. It is a real change (CI excludes zero) but
  specialization is not automatically unfavourable; the notebook flags it as a pattern to
  monitor, not a defect.
- That low dispersion is *good*. An animal weak in all six domains also has low dispersion.
- That any minimum threshold (RI or EI) *should* be implemented. Thresholds are flagged as
  values to simulate in a selection-index model first.
- That these are population genetic trends. They are cross-sectional comparisons of
  surviving animals grouped by birth year, evaluated on one date, not model-estimated trends.
- That selection *caused* any dispersion or distribution change. All statements are
  associational; management, survivorship and cohort composition are uncontrolled.
- Any causal or mechanistic claim about why the functional domains declined.
